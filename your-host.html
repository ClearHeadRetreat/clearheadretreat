Being straight about it

What the work cost him

Physical work is honest work, but it takes payment in instalments. By [EDIT: age or year], Wade's body was giving him the bill: [EDIT: the specifics, e.g. "a back that seized without warning, both knees gone, and shoulders that ached from the moment he woke up"].

The part nobody talks about is what it does to your head. Pain wears you down. Being tired all the time makes you short with the people you love. You keep going because stopping isn't an option when you are the business, and slowly you turn into someone you don't much like.

Nothing about it was dramatic. That's exactly the problem with it. There's no single moment that makes you go and get help, just years of a slow grind that you tell yourself is normal, because for the men around you it is.

The turning point

The moment it changed

[EDIT: the moment, in a few plain sentences. What happened, where he was, what he realised. For example: "It was a Tuesday, on a job in the rain, and he sat in the ute for forty minutes because he couldn't make himself get out of it. Not injured. Not sick. Just done."]

What pulled him back wasn't clever, and it wasn't a programme. It was time outside, a bit of physical work he actually chose, decent food, sleep, and enough quiet to hear himself think. It took months, not a weekend, but it started with getting out of the same four walls and being somewhere that put things back in proportion.

When he went looking for a place that did exactly that for other men, everything he found was either clinical, expensive, full of jargon, or a hundred people in a hall being told to open up on cue. Nothing that just gave a man a few days in good country with someone who understood.

So he built it. Turangi, because the hills and the river do half the work. A small group, because you can't hide in one that size and nobody gets treated like a delegate. No fixed programme, because what one man needs from time off is rarely what the next one needs.

You can't fix a life in a weekend. You can get your head clear enough to see it straight.

Being straight with you

What this isn't

Wade isn't a therapist or a counsellor, and Clear Head Retreat isn't treatment. It's a considered, hosted experience for men who are basically okay but overloaded, and who want room to reset.

If you're in real crisis, it wouldn't be fair to pretend a few days away is the answer. Part of the short conversation before you come is both sides being honest about whether this is the right fit. If it isn't, we'll happily point you somewhere that is.

In New Zealand you can call or text 1737 any time to talk with a trained counsellor, or contact your GP for support.
